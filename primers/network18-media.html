00

The 30-second snapshot

What you're actually buying, before we go deep.

Network18 Media & Investments Ltd is the listed media holding company of the Reliance group. Strip away the corporate plumbing and it holds two very different things. First, an operating news-and-digital business it runs day to day: the TV18 news bouquet — CNBC-TV18 and CNBC Awaaz in business news, CNN-News18 and the News18 family of regional-language channels — plus a large digital estate led by Moneycontrol (India's most-used financial portal), News18.com, Firstpost and Forbes India. Second, a passive minority stake in JioStar, the Disney-Star entertainment colossus formed in 2024, which sits on the balance sheet as an investment rather than a business Network18 operates.

Three facts frame everything that follows:

  • The operating business is a news house, and news is a hard business. Indian television news is advertising-driven, fiercely fragmented across dozens of channels and languages, and structurally low-margin — most of the time it barely breaks even or loses money. Network18's continuing news-and-digital operations ran an EBITDA margin of only about 2% in FY26. The prize inside it is digital — Moneycontrol's subscriptions and reach — but turning eyeballs into durable profit is the industry's unsolved problem.
  • The headline numbers just went through a cliff — for a structural reason, not a collapse. Consolidated revenue fell from ~₹9,297 Cr (FY24) to ~₹2,121 Cr (FY26). Network18 did not lose two-thirds of its business; its entertainment arm (Viacom18 — Colors, the movies and streaming) was merged out into JioStar in late 2024 and stopped being consolidated. What remains on the top line is the pure news-and-digital company.
  • The value question is unusual: how much of the share price is the newsroom, and how much is the hidden stake? The stock trades below book value (~0.85×) at a market cap around ₹4,200 Cr. A large slice of that book is the carrying value of the JioStar-linked investment — a stake in a business generating tens of thousands of crore in revenue that Network18 no longer runs but still owns a sliver of. The whole debate is a sum-of-the-parts one.
In plain terms

Network18 is really two companies wearing one ticker. One is a working newsroom — screens, anchors, reporters, a giant news website — that struggles to make money because Indian TV news is a crowded, ad-funded slog. The other is a quiet shareholding certificate: a minority interest in JioStar, the merged Disney-Star entertainment empire that carries the IPL, Hotstar and Star's channels, which Network18 helped build but handed operational control of to Reliance. When you buy the share you are buying both at once — a loss-making media operator and a passive claim on an entertainment giant — and most of the argument about the stock is about how to price that second, invisible half.

01

How a news-media business earns its money

No media background needed — just the handful of terms and revenue lines that run the sector.

Before Network18's specifics make sense, you need the basic machinery of an Indian news company. Unlike a factory that sells a physical product, a news operation sells attention — it gathers an audience with journalism, then earns from that audience two ways: advertisers pay to reach it, and (increasingly) some of the audience pays directly for premium content. Everything in this primer is a variation on that one loop.

The only media terms you need
TermWhat it means (and why it matters here)
Advertising revenueMoney brands pay to place ads around content — TV spots, website banners, video pre-rolls. The dominant income line for news, and the most cyclical: it rises and falls with the ad market, elections and the economy.
Subscription revenueMoney the audience pays directly — cable/DTH carriage fees on the TV side, and paid digital memberships like Moneycontrol Pro. Prized because it is recurring and less ad-cycle-dependent. The growth engine, but small today.
Viewership / audience shareThe percentage of a genre's viewers a channel captures (e.g. business news, English news). Higher share means an advertiser must buy you — it is the currency that sets ad rates. Network18 leads several news genres.
Linear TV vs digitalLinear = traditional scheduled broadcast on a TV set. Digital = websites, apps and streaming video watched on phones. Audiences and ad money are migrating from the first to the second — the sector's central shift.
Broadcaster vs holdcoA pure broadcaster (Zee, Sun) owns and runs channels. A holding company like Network18 owns operating media businesses and financial stakes in others — so its value is a sum of parts, not a single P&L.
The dependency that shapes the whole sector: news lives on the ad cycle

Indian news media is overwhelmingly advertising-funded, and that single fact explains most of its pain. Ad budgets are discretionary — they surge in an election year or a strong economy and get cut first in a downturn. Dozens of channels chase the same ad rupee in every language, so pricing power is thin. And unlike entertainment, news content ages in hours, so there is little re-sale value. The escape route the whole industry is chasing is direct payment from the audience — subscriptions and memberships that do not depend on the ad weather. Network18's most valuable idea, Moneycontrol Pro, is precisely a bet on that escape.

Where a news company's rupee comes from

For a business-and-general news operator like Network18, income arrives through a few channels with very different quality. Advertising is the biggest but the most volatile; subscription is smaller but far stickier; and a slice comes from syndication, events and other services.

02

Who really owns it: the Reliance question

Not a Reliance subsidiary in name, but controlled by it through a trust — and that changes how you read everything.

Network18 is controlled by Reliance Industries, but not in the straightforward way you might assume. Reliance holds its stake through the Independent Media Trust, of which Reliance is the sole beneficiary. That structure — a trust rather than a direct promoter holding — dates back to Reliance's takeover of the group in 2014, and it is worth understanding because it sits behind the biggest strategic decisions the company makes.

How the group got here

  • 1996–2005
    The Network18 group grows under founder Raghav Bahl into India's most prominent business-news house — CNBC-TV18, then CNBC Awaaz — and expands into general and regional news and digital.
  • 2014
    Reliance Industries takes control of Network18 and TV18 through the Independent Media Trust — a landmark moment that put India's largest company behind a major news and entertainment network.
  • 2018–2022
    Under Reliance, the entertainment arm Viacom18 (Colors, MTV, the movie studio, and streaming as Voot, later JioCinema) scales up, backed by heavy investment and the Jio distribution muscle.
  • Nov 2024
    Viacom18's media & streaming assets are merged into Disney's Star India to form JioStar, a Reliance-controlled entertainment giant. Network18 is left holding a minority stake in the combined entity and deconsolidates the entertainment business.
  • 2023–2025
    Network18 announces a scheme to merge subsidiary TV18 Broadcast and e-Eighteen.com (Moneycontrol) into Network18 itself — collapsing three listed/holding layers into one news-and-digital company. NCLT approval secured; final regulatory nods in progress.
  • FY26
    First clean post-split year: revenue ~₹2,121 Cr, return to profit (~₹155 Cr) after a ~₹1,777 Cr FY25 loss; TV news revenue +23%; Moneycontrol Pro past 1 million paid subscribers; News18 Lokmat fully acquired.

Ownership and structure

  • Ultimate controller: Reliance Industries, via the Independent Media Trust, holds the promoter stake — a large majority of the company. The balance is public float. Confirm the exact percentage in the latest shareholding filing, as group restructurings periodically move the holding.
  • Key operating subsidiary: TV18 Broadcast Ltd — the listed arm that houses the television news channels. Network18 is its parent; the pending merger folds it (and Moneycontrol's holding company, E18) directly into Network18.
  • The strategic asset: a minority interest in the JioStar joint venture — the merged Disney-Star entertainment business — carried as an investment, not consolidated. This is the "hidden" half of the company (section 06).
Why the Reliance-through-a-trust structure matters: control by India's largest and best-capitalised group is a double edge for a minority shareholder. On one side it means deep pockets, distribution via Jio, and a seat at the table of every group media deal — Network18's JioStar stake exists because it was inside the Reliance media orbit. On the other, the same parent runs group-level restructurings (the Viacom18-Disney merger, the TV18 amalgamation, stake reshuffles) whose terms are set above the listed company's head. Minority investors here are along for a ride steered by a controlling owner with its own group-wide agenda — the upside and the governance question are the same fact.
03

Two Network18s in one share

The single most important idea in the investment case — and it is specific to a media holding company mid-restructure.

Most people read Network18 as "a news channel company" and stop there. That misses half the balance sheet. A single Network18 share is a claim on two structurally different things: an operating news-and-digital business that flows through the income statement, and a passive entertainment stake that mostly does not. Getting this split straight is the whole trick to understanding the company — and it is why a plain P/E multiple tells you almost nothing here.

Half A is what the newsroom does; Half B is what the company owns. Revenue and margins describe only Half A. The market cap has to pay for both — which is why the value debate is a sum-of-parts, not a single earnings multiple.

The consequence is that Network18's income statement and its balance sheet tell two different stories. The income statement is Half A: a few thousand crore of news-and-digital revenue at wafer-thin margins. The balance sheet carries Half B: the investment value of the JioStar stake, which barely touches revenue but can be a large fraction of the company's net worth. This is exactly why the stock trades on price-to-book logic (what are the assets worth?) more than price-to-earnings logic (what does it earn?) — earnings capture only one of the two halves.

The device that only fits this company: read the P&L and the balance sheet as two different assets

Here is the mental model to carry through the rest of this primer. Everything on the revenue line is Half A — the operated news-and-digital business, and the ~2% margin, the +23% TV-news growth, the Moneycontrol subscriber count all describe only that half. Much of the equity value is Half B — the JioStar stake, which shows up as an investment and a share of associate profit, not as sales. So a headline like "revenue fell 66%" is a statement about Half A's accounting boundary changing, not about the company shrinking; and a valuation like "0.85× book" is mostly a statement about how the market is discounting Half B. Try to value Network18 on a single metric and you will misprice it — you have to add the two halves separately.

04

The crown jewels: Moneycontrol and CNBC-TV18

Inside the loss-making shell sit two genuinely dominant franchises — this is where the quality is.

It would be easy to dismiss a company that loses money as low-quality throughout. That would be a mistake here. Network18 owns two of the strongest single franchises in Indian media: Moneycontrol, the country's most-used financial-information platform, and CNBC-TV18, the dominant business-news channel. Their strength is what makes the loss-making frustrating — and what gives the digital pivot something real to build on.

Moneycontrol — the digital asset that could stand alone

Moneycontrol is the flagship of Network18's digital estate: India's leading destination for stock quotes, company data, market news and personal-finance tools, used by retail investors as a daily utility. Crucially, it is one of the few Indian news properties to have built a real paid subscription product — Moneycontrol Pro — which crossed over 1 million paying subscribers, ranking it the leading subscription-based financial-content platform in India. In an industry that mostly gives content away for ad pennies, a million people paying for premium market content is a structurally different, higher-quality income stream — and it sits alongside emerging fintech-adjacent features (data, tools, transactions) that deepen the utility.

CNBC-TV18 and the news bouquet — dominance without profit

On the broadcast side, Network18's channels lead their genres. CNBC-TV18 commands the overwhelming majority of English business-news viewership; CNBC Awaaz leads Hindi business news; CNN-News18 is a top English general-news channel; and News18 India plus a wide bouquet of regional-language channels reach across the Hindi belt and the states. Leadership in viewership share is real and valuable — it forces advertisers to buy these channels — yet, as the next section explains, genre leadership in Indian TV news does not reliably convert into profit.

Why the crown jewels don't rescue the P&L on their own

A first-time reader assumes that owning the #1 business-news channel and the #1 financial portal must mean fat profits. It does not — and understanding why is the crux. CNBC-TV18 dominates a genre that is prestigious but small and premium: business-news audiences are valuable per head but few in number, so the absolute ad pool is limited. Moneycontrol reaches enormous scale, but most of that reach is monetised at low digital ad rates; the million Pro subscribers are the high-quality slice, not the whole. So Network18 has trophy assets whose influence vastly exceeds their current cash generation. The bull case is that digital subscription and data can finally close that gap; the bear case is that it has stayed open for years.

05

The economics of Indian TV news — why leadership loses money

The structural reason a channel can top its genre and still bleed cash — faced squarely.

Indian television news is one of the toughest businesses in media, and Network18 lives inside it. The problem is not execution; it is structure. A combination of fragmentation, a fixed cost base, cyclical ad revenue and a politically sensitive product means that even genre leaders routinely run at losses or razor-thin margins. This is the sober core of the operating story, and it must be faced squarely rather than waved away.

Why TV news is structurally hard to profit from
ForceWhat it isHow it squeezes the maker
FragmentationHundreds of news channels across languages and states, many run for influence rather than profit.Splinters viewership and ad rates; no single channel can command pricing power the way a hit entertainment show can.
High fixed cost24×7 newsgathering — bureaus, reporters, satellite feeds, studios — costs the same whether ad revenue is up or down.Operating leverage cuts both ways; in a weak ad year the fixed newsroom cost turns a small margin into a loss fast.
Ad-cycle dependenceRevenue leans on advertising, which spikes around elections and a strong economy and is cut early in a slowdown.Earnings are lumpy and hard to forecast; a soft ad market can wipe out a year regardless of ratings.
Perishable productNews has almost no library or re-run value, unlike a movie or a serial.Every rupee of content spend is consumed immediately — no back-catalogue annuity to smooth the cycle.

Put those together and you get the sector's signature: a channel can win its genre and still lose money, because winning does not fix the fixed-cost-plus-cyclical-ad structure underneath. Network18's continuing news-and-digital operations ran an EBITDA margin of roughly 2% in FY26 — leadership across multiple genres translating into barely-there operating profit. The FY26 return to a net profit owed as much to the balance-sheet reset after the entertainment split and to share-of-associate contributions as to the newsroom suddenly minting cash.

The honest read on the operating business

A serious investor should not pretend the newsroom is a great business — it isn't, and years of thin-or-negative margins are the evidence. Network18 runs some of the most influential news brands in India and has still struggled to earn a consistent operating profit from them, because the structure of Indian TV news punishes even the leaders. The realistic case for the operating half is not that it becomes highly profitable, but that digital subscription and lower-cost distribution drag it toward sustained breakeven-plus while its influence is monetised in new ways. Anyone underwriting this stock on the operating business alone is underwriting a turnaround in one of media's hardest categories — which is exactly why the second half, the JioStar stake, carries so much of the argument.

06

The JioStar stake: the giant the newsroom quietly carries

The fact that surprises almost everyone looking at this small-cap for the first time.

Here is what a casual reader never sees coming. This modestly-sized news company — a few thousand crore of revenue, a market cap around ₹4,200 Cr — holds a minority stake in JioStar, India's largest entertainment company, an entity with pro-forma revenue in the region of ₹26,000 Cr. Network18 does not run JioStar; Reliance and Disney do. But it owns a slice of it, and that slice is one of the most valuable things on the balance sheet — hiding in plain sight behind the newsroom.

The chain runs like this. When Viacom18's media and streaming assets merged with Disney's Star India in November 2024 to form JioStar, Reliance emerged with control (about 63%) and Disney with the rest (about 37%). Reliance's holding runs partly directly and partly through Viacom18, which holds roughly 47% of JioStar. Network18, in turn, owns about 13.5% of Viacom18. Multiply the two and Network18's look-through economic interest in JioStar is on the order of ~6% — a minority, but a minority of an entertainment giant that owns the IPL digital rights, Hotstar/JioCinema and Star's channel bouquet.

The counter-intuitive truth to sit with

Network18's most valuable single asset may be something it neither operates nor consolidates. The newsroom is what the company does; the JioStar stake is a large part of what it is worth. That inversion is why the revenue line and the value line point in different directions — revenue is small and shrank on paper, while the balance sheet quietly holds a claim on one of India's biggest media businesses. It also means the stock carries a dependency most investors never price: the value of Network18's equity moves with how JioStar performs and how it is eventually valued — an entity whose accounts this company does not even fully see. You are buying a newsroom and, stapled to it, a minority lottery ticket on Indian streaming and sport.

Why this reframes the whole company: the JioStar stake turns Network18 from a simple media operator into a holding company with an embedded, non-controlled asset. That is powerful — few small-caps offer a back-door sliver of an ₹8.5-billion-dollar entertainment JV — but it comes with holding-company baggage: the stake is illiquid, its value is set by group decisions, there is no dividend stream a minority can count on, and the market habitually applies a holding-company discount to assets a shareholder cannot directly touch. The stake is the upside and the reason the stock lingers below book.
07

The great simplification: folding three layers into one

The restructuring that is trying to turn a confusing holding structure into a single, cleaner media company.

For years the group's news and digital assets were spread across separate listed and unlisted layers — Network18 the holdco, TV18 Broadcast the listed television arm, and e-Eighteen.com (E18) the private company that houses Moneycontrol. That structure created cross-holdings, minority leakage and a genuinely confusing corporate map. The ongoing merger of TV18 and E18 into Network18 is the group's attempt to collapse those layers into one platform-agnostic news-and-digital company.

The logic is straightforward and, on paper, value-accretive. Merging TV18 into its parent ends the parent-subsidiary minority split, so a Network18 shareholder owns the television channels and Moneycontrol directly rather than through a chain. It removes the awkwardness of a listed subsidiary (TV18) inside a listed parent. And it lets the company present a single, unified "news + digital" entity — TV channels and websites under one roof — which fits the reality that audiences no longer separate a "TV company" from a "web company." The scheme received NCLT approval, with final regulatory clearances (including from the information-and-broadcasting ministry) the remaining gate.

Why simplification is worth doing even without new revenue

A merger like this does not add a rupee of sales, so why bother? Because structure itself carries value in a holding company. Every layer between a shareholder and the underlying asset invites a discount — for minority leakage, for complexity, for the sense that value can be shuffled between entities. By pulling TV18 and Moneycontrol directly under one listed roof, Network18 removes reasons for that discount and makes the company legible: one entity, one set of accounts, one claim on the news-and-digital business. It also creates a cleaner vehicle should the group ever want to spotlight the digital assets. Simplification is not a growth lever; it is a discount-closing lever — and for a stock trading below book, closing the discount is the point.

08

The digital pivot — where the future has to come from

Linear TV is a mature, ad-cyclical base; the growth and the higher-quality money are online.

If the operating business has a path to being worth more than its thin margins suggest, that path runs through digital. Audiences and, more slowly, advertising are migrating from the television set to the phone. Network18's digital estate — Moneycontrol, News18.com across many languages, Firstpost — is already one of India's largest online news networks, reaching roughly 195 million unique visitors a month and about 36% of the digital-news audience. The strategic question is whether that reach can be monetised at a quality above low digital ad rates.

Two levers matter most. The first is subscription: Moneycontrol Pro's million-plus paying members prove that Indian audiences will pay for premium financial content, and every incremental subscriber is recurring, high-margin revenue that does not depend on the ad cycle. The second is fintech-adjacency — turning a finance-information audience into users of data, tools and transaction features, which are worth far more per user than an ad impression. A finance portal that people already open daily to check portfolios is unusually well placed to layer paid utility on top of free news.

The unglamorous truth about the pivot

The digital story is real but it is not a magic wand. Digital advertising in India is dominated by global platforms that take the lion's share of every online ad rupee, so a news publisher's ad monetisation online is lower per view than the old TV economics, not higher. That is why subscription and paid utility, not digital ads, are the only route to better margins — the company has to convert a small, high-intent slice of a 195-million audience into payers, again and again. Moneycontrol Pro shows it can be done; the open question is how far it scales before the growth in paid members flattens. The digital pivot is less a switch to flip than a subscriber-by-subscriber grind, and it is the single operational metric worth watching.

09

The rivals: a crowded newsroom and an open streaming front

Network18 fights on two fronts — for news audiences and, through its stake, in the streaming war it no longer runs.

Because Network18 straddles news operations and an entertainment stake, its competitive set is unusually wide. In its operated business it competes with other listed and unlisted news broadcasters and a swarm of digital-news publishers. Through its JioStar stake it is exposed to the streaming and general-entertainment war — a fight it is a passive participant in. Understanding both sides clarifies where the company stands.

The competitive shape to hold in mind

Two contrasts stand out. First, in news, Network18's problem is not a lack of audience — it leads several genres — but a lack of pricing power in a market splintered across Times Network, Republic, NDTV, TV Today, Zee's news arm and hundreds of regional channels; leadership in a fragmented, ad-funded genre does not pay like leadership in entertainment does, which is exactly why Sun TV and TV Today out-earn it. Second, in entertainment, Network18 is no longer a competitor at all — it is a shareholder watching JioStar battle Netflix and Amazon from the stands. Its fortunes there ride entirely on a business it helped create but handed to its parent to run.

The stock's ~0.85× price-to-book tells you the market is valuing this on what it owns — including the JioStar stake — and applying a discount, rather than on what the newsroom earns.

An earnings multiple is the wrong tool here, because earnings capture only the operated half. The number that proves or breaks the case is the holding-company discount — specifically, the gap between Network18's ~₹4,200 Cr market cap and a defensible sum-of-parts value of its two crown assets: the look-through JioStar stake (roughly 6% of a ~₹26,000 Cr-revenue entertainment leader) plus a standalone value for Moneycontrol as a million-subscriber financial platform. If those two alone are worth close to or more than the whole market cap, the newsroom and every other asset are being handed to a buyer for free, and any event that crystallises the JioStar value — a JioStar listing, a stake sale, a clean holdco simplification — closes the discount. If JioStar underperforms or its value stays locked behind the group structure indefinitely, the discount persists and the thin-margin newsroom is what the market keeps paying for. Sum the parts, subtract the discount — that is the scoreboard, not a P/E.

The future, the risks, and the bull/bear

A below-book media holdco with dominant news brands, a subscription bright spot, and a locked-up entertainment stake.

Network18 enters FY27 as a simpler, cleaner company than it was two years ago: entertainment hived off into JioStar, the news-and-digital layers folding into one, and a return to reported profit. What it cannot promise is that the market ever pays for its best asset — a JioStar stake it owns but does not control, whose value depends on Reliance's decisions and a possible future listing. That tension frames both cases.

The bull case

  • Below book, with hidden value — ~0.85× book, where much of that book is a look-through stake in India's largest entertainment company; the assets may be worth more than the price.
  • Genuinely dominant franchises — CNBC-TV18 owns business news, Moneycontrol owns retail-investor mind-share, and Moneycontrol Pro has 1M+ paying members proving digital monetisation works.
  • A crystallisation catalyst exists — a JioStar listing, a stake monetisation, or a clean holdco simplification could close the discount in a single event.
  • Reliance backing — deep capital, Jio distribution, and a seat at every group media deal reduce funding and survival risk.
  • Digital + fintech optionality — a 195M-visitor finance-heavy audience is unusually well placed to layer paid data and transaction features on top of news.

The bear case

  • The newsroom barely earns — ~2% margins in a fragmented, ad-cyclical, high-fixed-cost genre; years of thin-or-negative profit show how hard this business is.
  • The stake is locked and non-controlled — value depends on JioStar's performance and Reliance's choices; a minority may wait indefinitely with no dividend and no control.
  • Holding-company discount can persist — the market has every reason to keep discounting illiquid, group-steered assets; below-book can stay below book for years.
  • Ad-cycle and fragmentation pressure — a soft ad market or intensifying news competition (Adani's NDTV, Republic, Times) squeezes the operated business directly.
  • Governance under a controlling group — related-party restructurings and stake reshuffles are decided above the listed company; minorities take the terms they are given.
The bottom line

Network18 is a rare thing: a small-cap news operator with trophy brands, wrapped around a minority claim on one of India's largest entertainment businesses, trading below the stated worth of its assets. The operated newsroom is a hard, low-margin business that even genre leadership has failed to make consistently profitable — its realistic job is to grind toward durable digital-subscription economics, one Moneycontrol Pro member at a time. The real prize, and the real uncertainty, is the JioStar stake: worth a great deal on paper, invisible in the revenue line, and monetisable only if the controlling group chooses to unlock it. The next concrete test is whether a value-crystallising event — a JioStar listing or the completion of the holdco simplification — arrives and narrows the discount to book. If it does, the market finally pays for the hidden half; if the stake stays locked behind the group structure, an investor is left holding a below-book newsroom and a certificate they cannot cash. The screens report the news; the balance sheet is where the story is really being written.
