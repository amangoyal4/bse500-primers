00

The 30-second snapshot

What you're actually looking at, before we go deep.

Info Edge is two very different companies wearing one share price. The first is a boring-but-brilliant online-classifieds operating business — four websites where people pay to be found: Naukri.com (jobs, the dominant #1), 99acres (property), Jeevansathi (matrimony) and Shiksha (education). The second is an accidental venture-capital firm that, over 15 years, turned tiny cheques into stakes in Zomato/Eternal and PB Fintech (Policybazaar) now worth tens of thousands of crores.

Three facts frame everything:

  • Naukri is the profit engine. The recruitment business (Naukri.com + Naukri Gulf + iimjobs, etc.) did ~₹2,374 Cr of standalone billings in FY26 at a ~58% operating margin — one of the highest-margin businesses in listed India. It funds everything else. But it is cyclical: when IT companies stop hiring, Naukri's growth slows fast (Q4 FY26 recruitment billings grew just ~9.5%).
  • The listed stakes can be worth more than the core. Info Edge's ~12.4% of Eternal (Zomato) and ~13% of PB Fintech, plus a 135-company startup portfolio, were marked at ~₹41,300 Cr in June 2026 — a large chunk of the company's own ~₹67,000 Cr market cap. This is why Info Edge is a sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) story, and why its headline P/E (~47×) is close to meaningless.
  • The reported profit is a mirage. Consolidated FY26 PAT was ~₹1,763 Cr — but a huge slice of that is non-cash fair-value gains and other income on those investments, not money the operating business earned. Q4 FY26 alone carried ~₹497 Cr of "other income." You cannot value Info Edge on its accounting profit; you have to value the parts.
In plain terms

Picture a toll booth on India's job market that quietly collects cash — that's Naukri. Now imagine the family that runs the toll booth also happened, on the side, to buy early shares in two of India's biggest internet IPOs. Info Edge is that family holding company: a real, cash-generating classifieds business plus a war-chest of listed and private startup stakes. The share price bounces around because it reflects both — the health of the job market and the daily stock prices of Zomato and Policybazaar. To understand the stock, you have to value the toll booth and the investment book separately, then subtract a "holding-company discount" because you own them through a listed wrapper.

Engine A — the classifieds operating business

  • What it is: four "horizontal + vertical" classifieds portals. People and companies pay to list and to be found — recruiters buy Naukri subscriptions to search CVs and post jobs; builders and brokers advertise on 99acres; and so on.
  • Why it's a great business: it's a network-effect marketplace. The most job-seekers go where the most jobs are; the most recruiters advertise where the most CVs are. Once a portal wins that flywheel (as Naukri has), it becomes very hard to dislodge and can charge premium prices at near-zero incremental cost.
  • The numbers: ~₹3,052 Cr standalone revenue in FY26, ~38% EBITDA margin, negligible debt, throwing off ~₹1,000 Cr of free cash flow a year.

Engine B — the investment holding company

  • What it is: a portfolio of equity stakes, some in listed companies (Eternal, PB Fintech) and most in private startups, held directly and through the Info Edge Ventures funds (a JV with Temasek).
  • Why it exists: Info Edge started making minority internet bets in 2007 because it understood consumer-internet businesses. Two of those bets — Zomato and Policybazaar — became giants.
  • The numbers: the whole book was fair-valued at ~₹41,300 Cr in the June 2026 shareholder letter, against ~₹4,900 Cr of cumulative invested capital — an 8.4× multiple (MOIC) on cost.
Why this matters so much: a normal company's value ≈ (profit × a P/E multiple). Info Edge's value ≈ (value of Engine A) + (market value of Engine B's stakes) − (a holding-company discount). If you apply an ordinary P/E to Info Edge's accounting profit, you will get a badly wrong answer, because that profit is polluted by non-cash gains on Engine B. Sections 06–10 unpack exactly how to value each piece.
02

Classifieds economics: billings, deferred revenue & why margins are so high

No finance degree required — just how a subscription-ad portal actually makes money.

A classifieds portal is, financially, a software subscription business dressed up as a media business. It builds one website, then sells access to it thousands of times over. That structure produces three features you need to understand: upfront billings, deferred revenue, and operating leverage.

Billings vs revenue — the most important distinction

When a recruiter buys a one-year Naukri subscription, they pay the whole amount upfront. That cash collected is called billing. But accounting rules say the company can only recognise that money as revenue gradually — one-twelfth per month as the service is delivered. The gap between "cash already collected" and "revenue recognised so far" sits on the balance sheet as deferred sales revenue (unearned income).

The two numbers analysts track — and why
TermWhat it means (and why it matters for Info Edge)
BillingsCash collected from customers this period. The leading indicator — it shows demand today. FY26 standalone billings were ~₹3,177 Cr (+10%); recruitment alone was ~₹2,374 Cr.
RevenueBillings recognised over the subscription's life. It lags billings, so it's smoother. FY26 standalone revenue was ~₹3,052 Cr (+15%).
Deferred revenueBilled-but-not-yet-earned cash sitting on the balance sheet. A growing deferred balance = healthy future revenue already banked. It's essentially an interest-free float customers hand Info Edge.
Operating (EBIT) marginWhat's left after costs. Because the website is a fixed cost and each new subscription is almost pure margin, recruitment runs at a ~58% operating margin — extraordinary.
Why billings can slow while revenue still looks fine

Because revenue is recognised with a lag, it keeps rising smoothly even after billings (real-time demand) weaken. So when the job market cools, you'll see it in billings first — which is exactly why the market reacted nervously to Info Edge's Q4 FY26 update: recruitment billings grew only ~9.5%, and the stock fell ~5% on the day even though reported revenue and profit looked healthy. Watch billings, not revenue, to read the cycle. A cheerful revenue line can be hiding a billings slowdown that will show up in revenue a few quarters later.

Why the margins are so high — the marketplace flywheel

  • Fixed cost, infinite copies. Building and running naukri.com costs roughly the same whether 10 or 10 million recruiters use it. Every extra subscription is nearly all profit.
  • Network effects create pricing power. Naukri has the most CVs, so recruiters must be there; recruiters being there attracts the most job-seekers. This two-sided lock-in lets Info Edge raise prices year after year — a big driver of billings growth on top of volume.
  • Cash-flow-positive by design. Customers pay upfront (billings) but the service is delivered over a year, so Info Edge collects cash before it incurs the cost of serving — a negative working-capital cycle that self-funds growth.
03

The company and who controls it

From a 1997 job-listings website to an internet holding company.

Info Edge is a founder-led company, run by the same two people who built it. Sanjeev Bikhchandani launched Naukri.com in 1997 — literally one of the first commercial websites in India — and, with Hitesh Oberoi (now MD & CEO), turned a single job board into a multi-portal, multi-decade compounding machine. That continuity of ownership and judgement is central to the investment case.

  • 2005

  • 2006

  • 2010

  • 2008–15

  • 2020
    Formalises the investing arm as Info Edge Ventures (JV with Temasek); launches Fund I, then Fund II (2024) and Fund III (2025, ~₹1,000 Cr).
  • 2021
    Zomato and PB Fintech both IPO — Info Edge's minority stakes are suddenly worth many thousands of crores, transforming its balance sheet and its identity.
  • FY26
    Consolidated revenue ~₹3,285 Cr; startup portfolio marked at ~₹41,300 Cr (8.4× cost); a 1:5 stock split completed; recruitment growth cools with the IT-hiring cycle.

Ownership & management

  • Promoters: founders Sanjeev Bikhchandani (Executive Vice-Chairman; ~24% personally) and Hitesh Oberoi (MD & CEO), with the promoter group holding ~37.5% — a large, aligned, unpledged founder stake.
  • Why founder control matters here: Info Edge's edge is capital allocation — deciding which internet businesses to build, buy or back. That is a judgement call best made by owners with skin in the game and a 25-year track record of getting it right (Zomato, Policybazaar).
  • Balance sheet: essentially net cash / debt-light (~₹259 Cr borrowings against tens of thousands of crores of investments), so the operating business self-funds and the investment book is unlevered.
Info Edge's real product isn't a website — it's the founders' ability to spot which consumer-internet business will win, then own a piece of it early and cheaply.

What makes Naukri so dominant

  • Traffic and mind-share leadership. Naukri has held the highest market share and brand awareness among Indian job-seekers and recruiters for 20+ years — commonly cited around a ~70–75% share of recruitment portal traffic. "Naukri" is effectively a synonym for online job search in India.
  • The CV database is the moat. Naukri's real asset is the largest, most current resume database in India. Recruiters pay for search access to it (Resdex) — a product no rival can replicate without an equally deep pool of active job-seekers, which they can't attract without the recruiters. Classic two-sided lock-in.
  • Premium pricing + upsell. Beyond CV search and job postings, Naukri sells recruiter analytics, employer branding, campus tools and paid job-seeker services — layering higher-value products on the same audience.
The one thing to watch: the IT-hiring cycle

Naukri's fortunes are tied to white-collar hiring, and especially IT-services hiring, which historically drives a big share of recruiter spend. When Indian IT firms freeze headcount (as in the 2023–25 slowdown), recruiters buy fewer Naukri subscriptions and billings growth slows sharply — in Q4 FY26, recruitment billings grew just ~9.5%, with management flagging "macro uncertainty and geopolitical headwinds," including a slowdown in the previously ~20%-growing Naukri Gulf business. Info Edge even publishes a monthly "Naukri JobSpeak" hiring index that the market uses as a real-time gauge of white-collar demand. The bull point: non-IT verticals (BFSI, healthcare, AI/ML roles up ~38% YoY) and pricing keep growth positive even in a soft cycle — but Naukri is unmistakably a cyclical business bolted to India's job market.

The venture engine: Info Edge as an investor

How a classifieds company accidentally built one of India's best VC track records.

Info Edge's second identity is a venture-capital operation that has quietly compounded capital at a rate most professional VCs would envy. Understanding this engine — how it invests, what it holds, and how the gains are (and aren't) real — is essential to valuing the stock.

How the investing is structured

  • Direct stakes (legacy). The oldest, most valuable bets — Zomato/Eternal and PB Fintech — are held directly on Info Edge's own balance sheet, dating to pre-2015 investments.
  • Info Edge Ventures (the funds). Since 2020, new investing runs through AIF funds — a JV with Singapore's Temasek — spanning Fund I (2020), Fund II (2024) and Fund III (2025, ~₹1,000 Cr commitment). External limited partners co-invest alongside Info Edge, so it earns on other people's capital too.
  • The playbook: early-stage, consumer-internet and now increasingly AI-native and deeptech bets — 54 AI/deeptech companies backed with ~₹1,003 Cr between 2020 and March 2026.

The company deliberately holds rather than dumps its winners, so most of the value is "on paper" until it chooses to sell. This is why the reported consolidated profit swings wildly quarter to quarter — it moves with Eternal's and PB Fintech's share prices, not with how many Naukri subscriptions were sold.

Why a ~1,000× return on Zomato is the whole point

The holdings are a blessing (huge stored value) and a source of volatility (the share price inherits two other companies' beta).
08

SOTP, NAV and the holding-company discount

The single most important valuation idea for this stock — explained from scratch.

You cannot value Info Edge with a P/E. You value it by sum-of-the-parts (SOTP): add up what each piece is worth, then apply a holding-company discount. Here is what those terms mean and why the market price diverges from the raw parts.

Step 1 — Sum of the parts (the "NAV")

SOTP means valuing each engine on its own and adding them:

  • Value of the core classifieds business (Engine A) — the recruitment franchise plus 99acres/Jeevansathi/Shiksha, valued on their own cash flows/margins (analysts typically ascribe a large standalone value to Naukri given its ~58% margins and dominance).
  • + Market value of the listed stakes — Info Edge's ~12.4% of Eternal and ~13% of PB Fintech, at today's stock prices (often taxed/discounted for capital-gains on a notional sale).
  • + Fair value of the private portfolio — the Info Edge Ventures book of 130+ startups (~₹41,300 Cr total book, less the listed portion).
  • + Net cash on the balance sheet.

Add those up and you get the company's Net Asset Value (NAV) — what all the parts are worth if sold at marked prices.

Step 2 — Apply the holding-company discount

Here's the twist that trips up newcomers: a listed holding company almost never trades at its full NAV. The market applies a "holdco discount" — typically 20–50% — so that Info Edge's market cap sits below the sum of its parts.

Why the discount exists (and it's rational)

Four reasons the market pays less than NAV for a holding company: (1) You can't get the assets out. If you want Zomato exposure, you'd buy Zomato directly and keep 100% of the upside; buying it through Info Edge means you're trapped behind the holdco. (2) Tax leakage. If Info Edge sold its Zomato stake, it would pay capital-gains tax, so the after-tax value to you is less than the headline mark. (3) Capital-allocation risk. The cash from any sale is controlled by management, who might reinvest it in something you didn't want. (4) Double-counting of costs and the value being spread across many hard-to-price private startups. Net effect: even if the parts are "worth" ₹X, the stock trades at ₹0.6–0.8X. This discount widening or narrowing is itself a swing factor in the share price — independent of both Naukri and Zomato.

Why the reported P/E is misleading: Info Edge's ~47× trailing P/E divides the market cap by an accounting profit (~₹1,763 Cr FY26) that is inflated by non-cash fair-value gains on the investment book. That profit isn't recurring operating earnings, so the "47×" is comparing apples to oranges. A better lens is: strip out the investment gains, value the core business on its own profit (a much smaller, cleaner number), and treat the stakes separately at market value. Do that, and the "expensive-looking" P/E dissolves into a core-business multiple plus a portfolio-at-NAV-minus-discount — which is how sophisticated investors actually frame the stock.

The AI question: threat, or the next moat?

The structural force hanging over every classifieds portal — and Info Edge sits right in it.

Every online-classifieds business now faces the same existential question: what happens when AI can do the matching? If a chatbot can find you a job, a house, a college or a partner directly, do you still need a portal? For a company whose four products are search-and-match marketplaces, this is the defining long-term debate — the equivalent of "China" for a solar company or "birth rate" for a children's hospital.

The bear reading — disintermediation

  • Discovery moves to chatbots. If students ask an AI which college to pick, or job-seekers ask it to draft applications, the top-of-funnel traffic that portals monetise via advertising could erode. Info Edge itself flagged AI-led search headwinds at Shiksha in FY26.
  • In-house AI recruiting. Some large employers are using AI to source and screen candidates directly, potentially reducing reliance on paid job-board subscriptions over time.
The bull reading — why Naukri may get stronger, not weaker

Naukri's moat is the thing AI needs most: proprietary, verified, real-time data. An AI matching engine is only as good as the candidate pool it can search — and the largest, freshest CV database in India sits inside Naukri, not on the open web a chatbot can scrape. So AI is arguably a tailwind for Naukri: it can layer AI matching, recommendations and screening on top of its unique dataset, selling recruiters a better product at higher prices, while a generic chatbot has no candidate database to work from. Info Edge is explicitly deploying AI across matching, recommendations and new AI-native features. The distinction is generic-information portals vs proprietary-data marketplaces: Shiksha (mostly public information) is vulnerable; Naukri (a data + transaction network no chatbot can replicate) is where AI deepens the moat rather than dissolving it. And note Info Edge is investing in 54 AI/deeptech startups — hedging the threat by owning the disruptors.

The honest tension: AI is simultaneously Info Edge's biggest long-term risk (to Shiksha, matrimony and the ad-funded discovery model) and potentially its biggest reinforcement (to Naukri's data moat). The outcome will differ by portal. Watch whether Naukri's pricing power and share hold as AI recruiting tools proliferate — that's the number that decides whether AI is friend or foe for the crown jewel.

How to actually value this stock

Forget the headline P/E. The disciplined approach: (1) value the operating business (Naukri + the three portals) on its own recurring cash flow and ~58%-margin recruitment engine; (2) add the market value of the Eternal and PB Fintech stakes (less notional capital-gains tax); (3) add the fair value of the private Info Edge Ventures book; (4) add net cash; (5) apply a holding-company discount. The gap between that SOTP number and the ~₹67,000 Cr market cap tells you whether the stock is cheap or dear on any given day — and that gap moves with three independent forces: Naukri's billings cycle, Eternal's/PB Fintech's share prices, and the size of the holdco discount itself.

11

The future, the risks, and the bull/bear

A rare business: a cash-compounding operator that is also a proven capital allocator.

Info Edge is one of the most unusual compounders in listed India — a dominant, high-margin operating franchise and a top-decile venture track record, run by aligned founders. The debate is about how to value the mix, how the cycle plays out, and how AI reshapes the moats.

The bull case

  • Naukri's dominance & margins — a ~70–75% share, ~58%-margin cash engine with real pricing power and a proprietary CV-database moat AI can't easily copy.
  • Portfolio optionality — ~₹41,300 Cr of marked stakes (Eternal, PB Fintech + 130 startups) on ~₹1,000 Cr of cost; a proven ability to find the next winner.
  • Second margin tailwind — 99acres turning EBIT-positive; Jeevansathi/Shiksha nearing break-even means group margins can keep rising.
  • Aligned founder-owners — 25-year capital-allocation track record; ~37.5% promoter stake, unpledged, debt-light.
  • Trades below NAV — the holdco discount means you may be buying the parts for less than they're marked.

The bear case

  • Cyclicality — Naukri is tied to IT/white-collar hiring; billings growth already cooled to ~9.5% in Q4 FY26 amid macro headwinds.
  • Borrowed volatility — the share price inherits Eternal's and PB Fintech's beta; a fall in those stocks hits Info Edge's NAV directly.
  • AI disintermediation — Shiksha already hurt by AI search; the ad-funded discovery model faces a structural question across portals.
  • Holdco discount can widen — sentiment, tax leakage and capital-allocation worries can push the stock further below NAV independent of fundamentals.
  • Marks aren't money — most portfolio value is unrealised fair value that can be written down; the reported P/E flatters the operating reality.
The bottom line

Info Edge is best understood as a high-quality classifieds compounder stapled to a listed venture fund. The operating business (Naukri especially) is genuinely excellent — dominant, high-margin, cash-generative, and, if anything, strengthened by AI where its data moat is deepest. The investment book has already created enormous value and offers continued optionality. The catches are that (a) the stock's day-to-day price is hostage to Zomato and Policybazaar as much as to the job market, (b) the accounting profit badly overstates the operating earnings, and (c) AI is a live, unresolved question for the ad-funded portals. The way to follow it: track Naukri billings (the cycle), the Eternal/PB Fintech stakes (the NAV), and the holdco discount (the sentiment) as three separate dials — because at any moment, the stock is the sum of all three.
