Anthem Biosciences, the picks-and-shovels play on global drug innovation

00

The 30-second snapshot

What you're looking at, before we go deep.

Anthem Biosciences is a Bengaluru-based CRDMO — a Contract Research, Development & Manufacturing Organisation. In plain English, it is the outsourced R&D lab and factory for the world's drug companies: global pharma and biotech firms hand Anthem their new drug molecules to research, develop the manufacturing process for, and then make at scale. Anthem doesn't sell branded drugs of its own — it sells services and molecules to the people who do. It is the "picks-and-shovels" way to invest in the global drug-innovation boom without betting on any single blockbuster.

Three facts frame everything:

  • A rare dual-chemistry platform. Most Indian CRDMOs do only small-molecule synthetic chemistry. Anthem does that and a large, complex fermentation / biology-based business — peptides (the GLP-1 "weight-loss drug" family), oligonucleotides, enzymes, probiotics, biotransformation. This integrated small + large molecule capability is its differentiation and its moat.
  • Industry-leading margins. FY26 revenue was ₹2,124 Cr at a 43.4% EBITDA margin — among the very highest in the entire global CDMO/CRDMO space — with ₹592 Cr net profit (up 31%), ~30% ROCE and an essentially debt-free balance sheet.
  • A hot IPO, a rich price. Anthem listed on 21 July 2025 at a ~27% premium after a ₹3,395 Cr, 100%-offer-for-sale IPO (67× oversubscribed). It now trades near ₹43,400 Cr market cap on ~70× earnings — a valuation that only makes sense if the growth and margins persist.
In plain terms

Inventing and manufacturing a modern drug is fiendishly hard, capital-hungry and specialised — so drug companies increasingly rent the capability instead of building it. Anthem is one of the best "rental" partners: give it a molecule at any stage — a fresh idea from a lab, a compound heading into clinical trials, or an approved drug that needs tonnes made every year — and Anthem can research it, figure out how to make it cleanly and cheaply, and then produce it. Because it can do both ordinary "chemistry" molecules and the trickier "biology-grown" ones, it wins work that narrower rivals can't touch — and it charges premium prices for that scarcity, which is why its profit margins are so unusually high.

01

CRDMO 101: renting the drug-making machine

No pharma degree required — just the four letters and why each one matters.

To understand Anthem you have to understand the business model it sits inside. A drug's journey from idea to pharmacy shelf has three expensive stages, and a CRDMO can be hired for any or all of them. The acronym spells out exactly what it does.

Decoding "CRDMO"
LetterWhat it means (and what Anthem sells)
C — ContractAnthem works on contract for a client who owns the drug. The client keeps the intellectual property and the profits from the finished medicine; Anthem is paid a fee/price for the work.
R — ResearchEarly-stage drug discovery: designing and screening molecules, medicinal chemistry, biology. Low revenue per project but it is the front door — a molecule discovered here can grow into decades of manufacturing work.
D — DevelopmentProcess development: figuring out how to actually make a molecule reliably, safely, cheaply and at scale (a lab recipe for grams is not a factory recipe for tonnes). This is deep know-how, and it locks the client in.
M — ManufacturingMaking the drug's active ingredient (API) and intermediates at commercial scale, batch after batch, to regulatory standard. This is the big, recurring, high-value revenue — and the stickiest.
Why "discovery to commercialisation" is the magic phrase

The whole point of an integrated CRDMO is that it can grab a molecule at the very start (research) and then ride it all the way to full-scale manufacturing years later. Because switching a manufacturing partner mid-stream is risky and requires re-validation with regulators, a client who lets Anthem develop the process tends to stay with Anthem to make the drug. So an early discovery project — cheap and low-margin today — is really an option on a decade of high-value manufacturing tomorrow. Anthem says it has taken 10 innovator molecules from discovery all the way to commercialisation, and now makes 14 commercial molecules in total. That "molecule funnel" — early projects maturing into commercial supply — is the engine of the whole business.

How this differs from a generics company

  • A generic drug maker (say a Sun or Cipla) copies off-patent drugs and sells its own branded/generic product to patients and pharmacies. It carries marketing, distribution and pricing risk, and competes on cost in a crowded market.
  • A CRDMO like Anthem sells a service + molecule to the innovator. It never markets a drug, never faces a patient, and bears no clinical-trial success/failure risk on the drug itself — it is paid whether or not the drug becomes a commercial hit (though bigger drugs mean bigger manufacturing volumes).
  • The result: a CRDMO is a higher-margin, stickier, lower-consumer-risk business than generics — closer to a specialised industrial-services firm than a drug brand. That is exactly why the market prizes it more richly.
02

The dual-chemistry moat

The single thing that separates Anthem from almost every Indian rival — read this once and the whole thesis clicks.

Drug molecules come in two fundamentally different families, and making each requires a completely different toolkit. Anthem's differentiation is that it built both — and then layered the harder "new modalities" on top. That combination is genuinely rare in India.

Most Indian CRDMOs sit only in the top bar (small-molecule chemistry). Anthem's fermentation depth is what wins the complex, high-value work.

Small molecules — the "chemistry" world

Small molecules are the classic drugs — aspirin, statins, most tablets. They are built atom-by-atom in reactors through synthetic organic chemistry, and are called NCEs (New Chemical Entities). This is the mainstream CRDMO business: reactors, solvents, purification, scale-up. Anthem is strong here — but so is much of India. On its own it wouldn't be special.

Large molecules — the "biology" world

Large molecules are grown, not chemically synthesised — made by living cells (microbes, enzymes) in fermentation tanks, a process closer to brewing than to chemistry. This family includes peptides (short protein chains — the GLP-1 diabetes/obesity drugs like semaglutide are peptides), oligonucleotides (short DNA/RNA strands used in a new wave of gene-silencing medicines), enzymes, probiotics and biotransformation (using enzymes to do chemistry cleanly). Building fermentation capability takes years, scarce microbiology talent and different equipment — which is precisely why few rivals have it.

Why doing BOTH is the moat

Imagine a global biotech with a pipeline that mixes a traditional tablet, a GLP-1 peptide and an oligonucleotide drug. A pure small-molecule CDMO can only bid for one of the three; Anthem can bid for all of them and be a one-stop partner. That breadth does three things: it wins share of a client's whole pipeline; it lets Anthem chase the fastest-growing "new modality" molecules (peptides & oligos) that most Indian rivals simply cannot make; and it makes the relationship far harder to displace. Anthem's own framing is that its early investment in fermentation and complex modalities puts it "alongside global peers like Lonza and Bachem" rather than the pack of Indian small-molecule shops. This integrated small + large + fermentation platform is the core of the investment case.

The nuance: "large molecule" here does not mean Anthem is a full biologics/antibody manufacturer competing with the likes of a monoclonal-antibody CDMO. Its strength is the fermentation-based and complex-synthesis modalities — peptides, oligonucleotides, enzymes, biotransformation, ADC linkers, RNAi and lipid platforms — the "new modalities" that sit between classic chemistry and full biologics. That is the differentiated middle ground it has staked out.
03

The two revenue engines

CRDMO services do the heavy lifting; specialty ingredients ride alongside on the same fermentation know-how.

Anthem reports two businesses. The dominant one is the CRDMO services engine just described. The second — often overlooked — is Specialty Ingredients: fermentation-based specialty products Anthem develops and sells as its own, a natural spin-off of the same biology muscle that powers the CRDMO work.

FY26: CRDMO ₹1,773 Cr (+17.7%), Specialty Ingredients ₹352 Cr. Within CRDMO, roughly ~71% is Development & Manufacturing and ~11% is early-stage R&D.

Engine 1 — CRDMO services (~83%)

  • Development & Manufacturing (~71% of total): the big money — process development plus commercial supply of APIs and intermediates. This is the recurring, sticky, high-value core.
  • Research / discovery (~11% of total): the funnel-filler — early projects that feed future manufacturing. Small today, strategic tomorrow.

Engine 2 — Specialty Ingredients (~17%)

  • What it is: fermentation-derived products Anthem owns and sells — probiotics, enzymes, biosimilar-adjacent products, peptides and nutritional actives — rather than making to a client's order.
  • Why it exists: it monetises Anthem's fermentation infrastructure a second time, keeps expensive tanks utilised, and adds a product-margin stream alongside the services stream.
  • Why it matters: it is a real second engine and a proof-point of the biology capability — but it is the smaller, slower-growing sibling; the market's excitement is squarely on CRDMO.
How the two engines reinforce each other

The clever part is that both engines run on the same fermentation platform. The tanks, the microbial strains, the process scientists that Anthem built for its CRDMO clients are the very same assets that let it manufacture probiotics and enzymes to sell as specialty ingredients. So capital spent once is earning twice, capacity stays fuller, and the biology know-how compounds. It is a genuinely integrated model rather than two bolted-together businesses.

04

The company and who controls it

Founder-built, promoter-heavy, PE-groomed — and only recently public.

Anthem is a founder-led business begun in 2006–07 by Ajay Bhardwaj — a life-sciences veteran with 40+ years in the field who had earlier been President at Biocon and worked at Max India. He and a small group of scientist co-founders built Anthem into a differentiated CRDMO over ~18 years before taking it public in July 2025.

Ownership & management

  • Promoters ~74.7%: a promoter group led by Ajay Bhardwaj (Chairman, MD & CEO) with scientist co-founders and family — a very high, aligned founder stake, with no shares pledged.
  • Institutions ~13%: DIIs (mutual funds) around 11–12% and FIIs a little over 1% as of Mar 2026. Anthem was among the most-added midcap stocks by mutual funds in early 2026 — a sign of strong domestic institutional conviction.
  • The 100%-OFS structure: the IPO raised no money for the company — it was entirely early investors (PE + founders) selling down. That's a double-edged signal: it says the business is self-funding and doesn't need cash, but it also means insiders were monetising at the top of a hot market.
  • Balance sheet: essentially debt-free, ~22% ROE and ~30% ROCE — high-quality, self-financing growth.

06

New modalities: where the growth is hiding

The structural tailwind that turns Anthem's fermentation bet into a growth story — its version of "the theme you can't ignore".

If dual-chemistry is the moat, "new modalities" are the reason the moat is about to pay off. The drug industry is shifting away from purely traditional tablets toward complex molecules — and the two hottest are peptides and oligonucleotides, exactly the fermentation/complex-synthesis modalities Anthem invested in early.

The peptide wave (GLP-1 and beyond)

The single biggest force in pharma right now is the GLP-1 class — the diabetes-and-obesity blockbusters (semaglutide, tirzepatide and their successors) that are among the best-selling drugs in history. GLP-1s are peptides: they are made by peptide synthesis and fermentation, not classic tablet chemistry. As demand explodes and patents on early molecules approach expiry, drug companies need enormous, specialised peptide-manufacturing capacity — and there is a global shortage of it. A CRDMO with real peptide capability, like Anthem, sits directly in the path of that demand.

The oligonucleotide wave

Right behind peptides are oligonucleotides — short strands of DNA/RNA that power a new generation of "gene-silencing" medicines (RNAi, antisense) for previously untreatable diseases. Like peptides, "oligos" need specialised synthesis and purification that few CRDMOs can do at scale. Together, peptides + oligonucleotides are sometimes called the "TIDES" and are one of the fastest-growing corners of pharma manufacturing.

The global peptide-and-oligonucleotide CDMO market is estimated to grow at low-double-digit rates toward ~$5–6bn by 2030 — the pond Anthem is fishing in.
Why this matters so much for Anthem specifically

Two things make new modalities the crux of the bull case. (1) Scarcity of capability. Very few Indian CRDMOs can make peptides and oligonucleotides at scale — so Anthem competes here against a handful of global specialists (Bachem, PolyPeptide, Lonza, CordenPharma) rather than the crowded pack of small-molecule shops, which supports premium pricing. (2) The funnel is loaded here. Anthem has been building peptide, oligo, ADC-linker, RNAi and lipid-platform capacity precisely so that when its clients' new-modality molecules move from clinic to market, Anthem is the one who makes them. Management frames these as becoming material revenue drivers as customer molecules reach approval. The risk: these are pipeline revenues — they depend on clients' drugs actually getting approved, which introduces clinical-attrition risk out of Anthem's control.

The honest caveat: "new modalities" is genuinely exciting, but a chunk of it is still future revenue riding on molecules that are in trials, not yet on the market. If key client drugs fail in the clinic or the peptide-capacity race attracts a flood of new global supply, the premium narrative softens. Watch how fast new-modality revenue actually converts from funnel to sales.
07

China+1 and the industry tailwind

The structural reason global pharma is sending work to India — Anthem's "macro" chapter.

Anthem doesn't operate in a vacuum: it is riding one of the biggest supply-chain shifts of the decade. For years, global pharma leaned heavily on China for its outsourced research and manufacturing. A mix of geopolitics, tariffs, COVID-era supply shocks and — critically — proposed US legislation targeting Chinese CDMOs has pushed Western drug companies to diversify away from China. India is the obvious "plus one".

What "China+1" actually means for a CRDMO

  • De-risking, not replacing: Western clients aren't necessarily leaving China overnight — they are adding a second, non-Chinese partner to de-risk their supply chain. India, with its deep chemistry talent, English-language contracting, and IP-protection track record, is the leading beneficiary.
  • The BIOSECURE effect: proposed US rules restricting federal-linked business with certain Chinese biotech/CDMO firms have accelerated the search for alternatives — a direct tailwind for credible Indian CRDMOs.
  • Anthem's fit: a proven, quality-accredited, dual-chemistry Indian partner with long Western-client relationships is exactly what a de-risking pharma buyer is looking for.
Why the tailwind is real but not automatic

China+1 is a genuine multi-year structural force — but it is a tailwind, not a guarantee. Winning a China+1 mandate still requires world-class quality, regulatory track record, capacity and price. Chinese CDMOs remain formidable, low-cost competitors, and the shift is gradual because moving a validated drug process to a new country is slow and cautious. The prize for Anthem is being on the shortlist when a global client decides to diversify; the risk is assuming the wave carries every Indian player equally (it doesn't — it rewards the differentiated ones). Anthem's dual-chemistry + new-modality capability is precisely what should let it win more than its fair share of that redistribution.

Regulatory backdrop: as an export-heavy API/manufacturing player, Anthem's facilities are subject to inspections by the US FDA, European authorities and others. A clean regulatory record is table stakes — and a serious inspection failure at a key plant is one of the sharper tail risks in this industry.

The rivals

A crowded field of Indian CRDMOs — and a handful of global new-modality specialists.

Anthem competes on two fronts: against Indian CRDMO peers for the broad outsourcing pie, and against global specialists for the high-end new-modality work. Its pitch is that it straddles both worlds better than most.

Where Anthem actually wins

Against the Indian pack, Anthem's edge is margin and modality breadth — its ~43% EBITDA margin is at or near the top of the group, and its fermentation/new-modality depth is rarer than most peers'. Against the global specialists, its edge is India's cost base and the China+1 tailwind. The uncomfortable truth is that everyone sees the same opportunity: Divi's, Syngene, Sai Life, Piramal and Cohance are all expanding into complex modalities too, and global players are adding capacity. So the competitive question for the next five years is whether Anthem's early lead in fermentation/new-modalities and its margin discipline let it keep winning the differentiated work as the field crowds in.

Why are the margins so high?

Three reasons stack up. (1) Mix. Anthem skews toward differentiated, hard-to-make molecules (fermentation, new modalities, complex synthesis) that command premium pricing versus commodity chemistry. (2) Integration & utilisation. Doing research, development and manufacturing under one roof, and running the same fermentation assets for both CRDMO and specialty ingredients, keeps capacity full and overhead spread thin. (3) India cost base + scale. World-class capability at Indian operating cost. The result is an EBITDA margin (~43%) that sits well above the typical CDMO's ~20–25%. That margin is the crown jewel of the story — and the number bears must argue will eventually normalise as competition and capex catch up.

Capacity and the growth runway

Reactors and fermenters are the growth lever — and Anthem is adding a lot of both.

For a manufacturing business, capacity is destiny: you can only grow revenue as fast as you can add (and fill) reactor and fermentation volume. Anthem runs three units today and is building a big fourth — the clearest, most tangible growth driver in the story.

Three stacked growth engines

  • Capacity — Unit IV and Unit III ramping: the raw ability to make (and bill for) more.
  • Funnel maturation — early-stage projects graduating into commercial manufacturing; the ~14 commercial molecules becoming more, plus deeper share of existing clients' pipelines.
  • Mix / new modalities — peptides, oligonucleotides and other high-value modalities lifting revenue per unit of capacity as those molecules reach market.
The next chapter is a race to fill new fermenters and reactors with the right molecules — capacity is being built ahead of the demand it is betting on.
The utilisation watch-item: big greenfield capacity, like any hospital bed or factory line, opens with full fixed costs and starts near-empty. If Unit IV fills quickly with high-value work, operating leverage lifts margins further; if demand lags the concrete, near-term margins and returns can dip while the new plant ramps. Utilisation of the new units is a key number to track post-FY26.

Valuation, risks and the bull/bear

A genuinely high-quality business — priced for near-flawless execution.

Anthem is, on the numbers, one of the highest-quality names in Indian pharma services: fast-growing, best-in-class margins, high returns, debt-free, and levered to two powerful themes (new modalities + China+1). The entire debate sits on valuation.

How to think about the ~70× multiple

At roughly 70× trailing earnings and ~14× book, Anthem is priced as a premium compounder, not a value stock. That price is defensible only if you believe several things persist for years: sector-leading margins hold near ~40%+, the new-modality funnel converts into real commercial revenue, China+1 keeps redirecting global work toward Anthem, and Unit IV fills profitably. Bulls argue a scarce, differentiated, debt-free CRDMO growing 15–20%+ with 40%+ margins genuinely deserves a scarcity premium. Bears argue the multiple leaves no room for error: any margin normalisation, a lumpy quarter from client concentration, a regulatory stumble, or slower new-modality conversion, and a 70× stock can de-rate sharply. Both can be right at different points — which is why margins, utilisation and new-modality revenue are the numbers to watch each quarter.

The bull case

  • Differentiated moat — rare dual small + large molecule + fermentation platform; few Indian rivals can match the new-modality depth.
  • Best-in-class margins — ~43% EBITDA, ~30% ROCE, debt-free; a genuinely high-quality financial profile.
  • Twin tailwinds — the peptide/oligo (new-modality) wave and China+1 supply-chain de-risking both point work toward Anthem.
  • Sticky funnel — ~12-year top-client relationships, discovery-to-commercial lock-in, a widening molecule funnel.
  • Capacity runway — Unit IV nearly doubles capacity, self-funded, ready for the demand it's betting on.

The bear case

  • Very rich valuation — ~70× earnings / ~14× book leaves little margin for error; vulnerable to de-rating.
  • Client concentration — top-5 customers ~71% of revenue; one large programme pausing hurts.
  • Pipeline / clinical-attrition risk — new-modality upside rides on client drugs actually getting approved — outside Anthem's control.
  • Crowding competition — Divi's, Syngene, Sai Life, Piramal, Cohance and global specialists all chasing the same complex work; margins could normalise.
  • Forex & regulatory — ~85% exports (currency swings) plus FDA/EU inspection risk at key plants; 100%-OFS IPO means no fresh growth capital was raised.
The bottom line

Anthem Biosciences is a structurally advantaged, exceptionally profitable CRDMO sitting at the intersection of two of pharma's strongest themes — the shift to complex new modalities and the diversification of supply chains away from China. The business quality is hard to argue with. The valuation is where the risk lives: near 70× earnings, the market is already paying for years of flawless execution. The story over the coming years will be written in a few numbers each quarter — the EBITDA margin (does ~43% hold or normalise?), new-modality revenue conversion (does the funnel become sales?), and Unit IV utilisation. Get those right and the compounding justifies the premium; stumble, and a 70× stock has a long way to fall.
