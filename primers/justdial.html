Justdial, a cash pile with a search engine attached

00

The 30-second snapshot

What you're looking at, before we go deep.

Just Dial Limited runs India's best-known local-search and business-listings platform — the service that started as a phone number you could ring to ask "where's the nearest plumber / caterer / dentist?", and grew into an app, a website and a database of 54.7 million business listings. It makes its money not from the people searching, who pay nothing, but from the businesses being searched for: roughly 6.3 lakh small and medium enterprises pay Just Dial for visibility and leads through "paid campaigns." Since 2021 the company has been controlled by Reliance Retail Ventures, part of the Reliance group, with founder V.S.S. Mani still running it.

Three facts frame everything:

  • It is a two-sided platform that monetises only one side. Consumers search for free; the money comes from advertisers. Just Dial's revenue is essentially advertising and paid-listing subscriptions sold to SMEs — a small shop pays a few thousand rupees a month to sit higher in results and receive buyer leads. In FY26 that engine produced ~₹1,214 Cr of revenue and a healthy ~29.5% operating margin. It is a genuinely profitable core.
  • Its balance sheet is stranger than its business. Just Dial sits on ~₹5,852 Cr of cash and investments — a hoard built up over a decade of a cash-generative model and never returned or deployed at scale. That pile is roughly equal to the company's entire stock-market value (~₹5,772 Cr). In effect, the market prices the cash at face value and throws in the operating business for nothing — the deep-value puzzle that dominates the investment debate.
  • Profit and business health have come apart. In FY26 the operating business grew — revenue +6.3%, operating pre-tax profit +9.3% — yet reported net profit fell ~15% to ~₹497 Cr. The reason is that nearly half of Just Dial's pre-tax profit is interest and treasury income on that cash pile, and when market yields and gains softened, headline profit dropped even as the actual business improved. You have to read the two apart.
In plain terms

Picture a company that is really two things stapled together. One is a modestly-growing advertising business: it maintains a giant directory of Indian businesses, lets anyone search it for free, and charges the businesses to stand out. The other is a very large, very conservative investment portfolio — over ₹5,800 crore of bank deposits, bonds and funds — that quietly earns interest each year. For long stretches the portfolio has thrown off almost as much pre-tax profit as the advertising engine itself. That is why Just Dial's share price behaves less like a growth-tech stock and more like a discount-to-cash puzzle: buy it near its own bank balance, and you are paying close to zero for whatever the search business is still worth. The catch is that "whatever it's worth" is exactly the thing under structural pressure.

01

Local-search 101: how a directory business works

No tech background needed — just the two sides of the platform and who pays.

A local-search platform is a modern Yellow Pages: a searchable index of local businesses that a consumer consults when they want to find and contact a service near them — a home tutor, a car mechanic, an event caterer, an interior designer. The platform's value is in the completeness of its listings and the traffic of people searching them. To follow Just Dial you need five ideas.

The only local-search terms you need
TermWhat it means (and why it matters here)
ListingA business entry in the database (name, category, address, phone, reviews). Just Dial carries 54.7 million of them. Most are free — the listing itself is bait to attract searchers; the money comes from a small paid slice.
Paid campaignA business that pays Just Dial for premium placement, a verified profile and buyer leads. About 6.3 lakh businesses do this — roughly 1.2% of all listings. This is the revenue base: everything hinges on growing this number and what each one pays.
Lead / enquiryA prospective customer's contact request routed to a business. Just Dial's core promise to an SME is qualified leads — a plumber pays to receive phone numbers of people looking for a plumber nearby.
TrafficUnique visitors searching the platform each period — 182.4 million in the March 2026 quarter. Traffic is the raw material: no searchers, no leads, no reason for a business to pay. Watch its direction closely.
ARPU / realisationAverage revenue per paying campaign. Roughly ₹19,000 a year (~₹1,600 a month) per SME. Revenue grows two ways only: more paying businesses, or more rupees from each — both hard-won.
The one dependency that shapes the whole model: it is an attention business, not a service business

Just Dial does not clean your house, deliver your food or fix your tap. It sells attention and intent — it captures a person at the exact moment they are looking to hire or buy locally, and rents that moment to a business willing to pay for it. That is a wonderful model when you own the moment: near-zero marginal cost, cash collected upfront, fat margins. But it is a fragile one, because the "moment of local intent" is the single most contested piece of real estate on the internet. Google owns it through Search and Maps; Zomato and Swiggy own it for food; specialist apps own it category by category. A horizontal directory's entire worth rests on remaining the place Indians go first to find a local business — and that is precisely what a decade of app-fragmentation has been chipping away.

The two sides, and who pays whom

  • The searcher (consumer) — uses the app, website or the legacy phone line to find a business. Pays nothing. Their numbers (traffic) and their intent are the product.
  • The listed business (free) — appears in the directory whether it pays or not. Free listings bulk up the database and improve search coverage, which draws more searchers.
  • The paying advertiser (SME) — the ~6.3 lakh businesses that buy campaigns for placement and leads. This is the only side that generates revenue. The whole art of the business is converting free listers and searcher-intent into paying advertisers.
02

How the money is made: campaigns, ARPU, and cash upfront

The single most important structural fact about the operating business — get this and the P&L follows.

Just Dial's revenue is almost entirely subscription-style advertising sold to small businesses. An SME signs up for a paid campaign — typically an annual package — and pays Just Dial, often upfront, for a year of premium placement and lead delivery. Two levers, and only two, move the top line: the number of paying campaigns and the average price each pays. Everything in the P&L flows from that simple multiplication.

Why "cash upfront" matters more than it looks

Because campaigns are typically sold as annual packages paid in advance, Just Dial collects the cash before it delivers the year of service. Accounting rules make it recognise that revenue evenly over the campaign, so the un-earned portion sits on the balance sheet as deferred revenue (a liability). This has two consequences a first-timer should grasp: first, the business is negative working capital — customers fund it, so it needs little capital to grow and generates cash faster than reported profit suggests. Second, deferred revenue is a useful leading indicator: if it is rising, advertisers are still signing annual deals; if it stalls, future revenue is about to.

The device that fits Just Dial specifically: revenue = campaigns × realisation, both grinding

The whole operating story reduces to one line — paid campaigns × revenue-per-campaign — and in FY26 both moved, but slowly. Paying campaigns rose about 3% to ~6.32 lakh; realisation per campaign crept up; and the product of the two delivered ~6.3% revenue growth. That is the true signature of this business today: a large, sticky base of small advertisers that expands a few percent a year, at a slowly rising price, throwing off high-margin cash. It is not a hyper-growth internet story and has not been one for years. The bull case is that this grind is durable and under-monetised; the bear case is that it is the quiet erosion of a directory being routed around — and the tell is whether that campaign count keeps climbing or one day rolls over.

The platform: listings, traffic, and the voice that won't die

Where the searchers actually are — and the surprising channel still handling millions of them.

Just Dial reaches its searchers through three surfaces — a mobile app, the website, and its original voice service (the human-operator phone line at 8888888888). The mix tells you how thoroughly India has gone mobile — and hides one genuinely surprising legacy.

Mobile now carries ~86% of all searches. But note the bottom bar: the original voice service — call a number, an operator finds you a business — still handles ~2.8% of traffic, roughly 5 million visits a quarter. The 1996 idea is quietly still alive inside the app-era company.

The bigger story in the traffic figure is its direction. Total traffic of 182.4 million was down ~4.7% year-on-year — searchers are, on the margin, arriving slightly less than they used to. For a business whose entire premise is being the place Indians go first to find a local service, a multi-quarter drift in traffic is the metric that should worry a bull most, because it is the raw material that everything downstream (leads, advertiser value, pricing power) depends on. Management has held revenue growing by adding listings and nudging advertiser realisation, but you cannot sell intent you are no longer capturing.

The database keeps swelling and revenue keeps grinding higher, but the number of people searching is falling. That gap — more listings, fewer searchers — is the central operational tension of the business, and why "is the directory still where people start?" is the question that matters.
The surprise a smart generalist misses

Everyone assumes Just Dial is a pure app company now — and 86% mobile says it mostly is. The overlooked fact is the opposite one: a company owned by Reliance, trading as an internet stock, still routes millions of searches a quarter through human telephone operators answering a 1996-vintage number. It is a living fossil of the original business model, kept alive because in parts of India a phone call still beats an app for a certain user. It costs money to run and it flatters no growth narrative — but it is a reminder that Just Dial's "moat" was always as much brand-recall of a phone number as it was technology, and brand-recall is exactly the asset that AI-era search erodes fastest.

04

The company, the founder, and who controls it now

A founder-built internet pioneer that is now a Reliance subsidiary in all but name.

Just Dial is a rare thing: a genuine first-generation Indian internet company, built by a single founder from a telephone line, that listed publicly, prospered, and then sold control to the country's largest conglomerate. Understanding that arc — and who sits on the cap table today — explains both the strategy and the market's ambivalence.

Timeline

  • 1996
    V.S.S. Mani founds Just Dial as a telephone-based local-search service — you call an operator and ask for a business. The memorable number 8888888888 becomes a household brand across urban India.
  • 2007
    justdial.com launches; the business begins shifting from voice to web, and later to a mobile app, tracking India's internet adoption.
  • 2013
    IPO on NSE & BSE (scrip 535648) — one of India's first pure-play internet listings, a landmark for the sector and a strong debut.
  • 2016–2019
    "Search Plus" and transaction-led experiments to move beyond listings into bookings and services; an app-first pivot to defend against fast-rising vertical apps. Results are mixed.
  • 2020
    JD Mart launched — a dedicated B2B marketplace to take on IndiaMART, leveraging Just Dial's business database.
  • Jul 2021
    Reliance Retail Ventures acquires control for ~₹5,700 Cr (including the open offer), taking a majority stake. Mani stays on as MD & CEO; Just Dial becomes part of the Reliance retail/O2O orbit.
  • FY26
    Revenue ~₹1,214 Cr (+6.3%), operating EBITDA ~₹357 Cr (29.5% margin), but PAT ~₹497 Cr (−15%) as treasury income eased; cash & investments ~₹5,852 Cr; traffic −4.7% YoY; CFO resignation disclosed at results.

Ownership & structure

  • Controlling promoter: Reliance Retail Ventures Ltd (RRVL), the retail holding arm of the Reliance group, is the controlling shareholder following its 2021 acquisition and open offer. The promoter group holds roughly 74% in total, with RRVL the dominant block. Investors should confirm the exact RRVL and promoter-group percentages in the latest shareholding filing — the promoter mix has shifted since the 2021 deal.
  • The founder: V.S.S. Mani remains Managing Director & CEO and retains a promoter-group holding — an unusual arrangement where the original founder still runs a business now owned by a much larger parent.
  • The float: the balance is public shareholding. A ~74% promoter stake means a thin free float, which magnifies price moves and keeps the discount-to-cash anomaly (section 05) from being easily arbitraged away.
Why the ownership defines the whole thesis: Just Dial is no longer a standalone bet on local search — it is a bet on what Reliance chooses to do with it. Reliance can treat it as a data-and-listings feeder for its own O2O (online-to-offline) and JioMart ambitions, deploy the giant cash pile, integrate it deeply, or simply let it run. The CFO's departure at the FY26 results is the kind of governance signal minority holders watch in a promoter-controlled company. The upside and the overhang both live in that parent relationship.
05

The cash mountain: worth more than the whole company

The single most important — and strangest — number in the investment case, and it is specific to Just Dial.

Here is the fact that stops every value investor who looks at Just Dial for the first time. The company holds about ₹5,852 Cr of cash and investments — bank deposits, bonds, mutual funds — built up over a decade of a cash-generative, low-capital model and never returned or spent at scale. Its entire market capitalisation is about ₹5,772 Cr. The cash on the balance sheet is larger than the price of the whole company. Buy every share, and the cash you'd inherit more than covers the cheque.

Strip the cash out of the market cap and the enterprise value — what the market pays for the actual operating business — is roughly zero, even slightly negative. A profitable advertising platform earning ~₹357 Cr of operating EBITDA is being handed to buyers for less than nothing, because the cash alone accounts for the price.

This is not an accident of one day's price; the cash pile has been growing for years and the stock has traded around it for years. And the pile keeps compounding — up from ~₹4,608 Cr at end-FY24 to ~₹5,279 Cr at end-FY25 to ~₹5,852 Cr at end-FY26 — because the operating business keeps generating cash the company neither reinvests heavily nor pays out.

The device only Just Dial fits: half the profit is a bond portfolio, not a business

Split FY26's pre-tax profit in two and the anomaly becomes concrete. The operating business earned about ₹303 Cr of pre-tax profit. The cash pile earned, through interest and treasury gains, about ₹313 Cr more — roughly half of the ~₹616 Cr total. So when people cheer or mourn Just Dial's "profit," they are half-talking about a search engine and half-talking about an interest-rate return on a ₹5,800 Cr deposit book. That is why FY26 net profit could fall 15% in a year the operating business grew: treasury income cooled, and the portfolio-half of earnings shrank. No amount of advertising growth shows through cleanly until you separate the two halves — the operating engine, and the giant piggy bank sitting beside it.

Which raises the question that hangs over the entire stock: why isn't the cash deployed or returned? The bull reads the setup as absurdly cheap — you buy the cash at par and get a profitable platform free, and any dividend, buyback or acquisition would crystallise value. The bear notes that the same discount has persisted for years precisely because the market doubts the cash will ever reach minority shareholders: a controlling promoter (Reliance) may prefer to keep the war chest for its own strategic uses, and a value trap is exactly what "cheap cash that never comes out" looks like. Both readings turn on the same lever — capital allocation by the parent.

How to hold this in your head: Just Dial is priced as though the operating business is worthless and the cash is worth every rupee. If you believe the search business has any durable value at all, the stock is cheap; if you believe the cash is permanently trapped and the business is in structural decline, the "value" is a mirage that could erode as the business does. The whole argument is that narrow.
06

JD Mart vs IndiaMART: the B2B pivot, and who actually leads

Just Dial's most-hyped growth bet — measured squarely against the incumbent it is chasing.

Alongside its consumer local-search business, Just Dial has pushed hard into B2B with JD Mart, a marketplace connecting wholesale buyers and suppliers. The pitch is natural: Just Dial already holds a vast database of Indian businesses, so extending it into a "find-a-supplier" marketplace looks like free adjacency. But it is essential to be clear-eyed about the competitive reality — Just Dial is the challenger here, not the leader.

B2B marketplace — the incumbent versus the challenger
DimensionIndiaMART (the leader)JD Mart (Just Dial)
PositionIndia's clear No.1 B2B marketplace — the default platform where buyers post requirements and suppliers pay for leads.A later entrant (2020) extending Just Dial's business database into B2B; a credible challenger, not the market-maker.
FocusPure-play B2B — the entire company is built around supplier discovery and buyer-lead generation.One line among several — B2B sits beside the core B2C local-search business that still dominates revenue.
MonetisationPaid supplier subscriptions; a large base of paying suppliers and years of B2B-specific product depth.Paid listings/leads, folded into Just Dial's overall campaign base; B2B-specific scale still building.
Market's verdictValued as a focused, cash-generative B2B leader — a materially larger market cap than Just Dial.Valued as part of a discount-to-cash local-search company; JD Mart is optionality, not the base case.

The distinction matters because B2B marketplaces have strong network effects: buyers go where the suppliers are, suppliers go where the buyers are, and that flywheel entrenches whoever got there first at scale. IndiaMART got there first and built a deep, paying supplier base; unseating an entrenched two-sided network is one of the harder tasks in internet businesses. JD Mart's advantage is Just Dial's existing database and brand; its disadvantage is that it is fighting a focused incumbent whose entire company is B2B while B2B is a side-project inside Just Dial.

The realistic way to weigh JD Mart

Treat JD Mart as a call on adjacency, not a second IndiaMART. If it works, Just Dial monetises its business database twice — once to consumers, once to wholesale buyers — and adds a growth leg the core lacks. If it stays sub-scale, it is a modest, tolerable investment that neither makes nor breaks the company, because the core B2C advertising engine and the cash pile still define the stock. What JD Mart is not, on current evidence, is a business about to overtake IndiaMART in B2B — and any thesis that leans on that should be viewed sceptically. The number to watch is JD Mart's paying-supplier traction disclosed over time, not the marketing claim of being "India's largest."

07

The disruption stack: who is eating local search

The structural question a directory business cannot dodge — and the new one AI just added.

The uncomfortable core of the bear case has nothing to do with the balance sheet and everything to do with the business's relevance. A horizontal directory is squeezed from several directions at once, and each has been getting stronger for a decade. This is the "why does the market value the business at zero?" question answered plainly.

The forces routing around a horizontal directory
DisruptorWhat it doesWhy it hurts Just Dial
Google Search & MapsAnswers "near me" queries directly, with reviews, hours, directions and a call button — for free, inside the world's default search box.The most serious threat. For a huge share of local-intent searches, Google is the directory now; Just Dial has to win the user before Google does.
Vertical appsZomato/Swiggy (food), Urban Company (home services), MagicBricks/NoBroker (property), Practo (doctors), and dozens more own a category each.They peel the most valuable, highest-intent verticals off a horizontal directory, leaving it the long tail of low-monetisation categories.
Quick-commerce & O2OBlinkit, Instamart, Zepto and retail platforms fulfil the need directly rather than just pointing to a business.Users who once searched "grocery near me" now open a delivery app — the intent never reaches a directory at all.
AI / conversational searchChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI overviews answer "find me a good X nearby" in natural language, synthesising results.The newest and most existential: if an AI assistant becomes the front door to local discovery, an intermediary listings site risks being disintermediated entirely.

This is why the ~4.7% traffic decline in section 03 is not a rounding error — it is the visible edge of a structural question. A directory monetises by standing between a searcher and a business. Every disruptor above either becomes the searcher's new starting point (Google, AI) or fulfils the need without a directory in the loop (vertical and quick-commerce apps). Just Dial's defence is real but bounded: deep coverage of India's long tail of small businesses, a trusted brand, verified listings and a lead-delivery machine tuned to SMEs that giant platforms under-serve. The question is whether that long-tail niche holds its value as the high-value head of local search migrates elsewhere.

The honest framing of the structural risk

A directory's worth is a function of being the first place people look. Google has been chipping at that for fifteen years; vertical apps for ten; AI search for two. None of them has killed Just Dial — the SME long tail, verified leads and brand recall are genuinely sticky, and 6.3 lakh businesses still pay because the leads still convert. But the trend line under "does the directory still capture the moment of local intent?" points one way, and it is the reason a profitable, cash-rich company trades at an enterprise value of roughly zero. The market is not saying the business earns nothing today; it is saying it doubts what those earnings are worth a decade out. That doubt is the whole discount.

08

The Reliance question: synergy or overhang?

The one relationship that can resolve the discount in either direction.

Because Reliance controls Just Dial, the parent's intentions matter more than any single operating metric. The 2021 acquisition was strategically logical — Reliance has vast O2O (online-to-offline) ambitions across JioMart, retail and its merchant network, and Just Dial's database of tens of millions of Indian businesses is a natural feeder for connecting Reliance's ecosystem to local commerce. But five years on, the market is still waiting to see the synergy show up in Just Dial's own numbers.

The two ways this can go

  • Synergy (the bull reading): Reliance integrates Just Dial's listings, leads and merchant relationships into its O2O and JioMart engine; deploys the ₹5,852 Cr cash pile into growth or returns it to shareholders; and uses group scale to defend local search against Google and the verticals. In this world the discount closes and Just Dial becomes a valuable node in a much larger commerce network.
  • Overhang (the bear reading): Reliance keeps Just Dial as a lightly-integrated, cash-rich subsidiary run for the parent's convenience; the cash stays put (a strategic reserve, not a shareholder asset); minority investors get little say; and the persistent discount is the market's price for that uncertainty. Governance signals — such as a CFO departure at the FY26 results — are read closely in this light.
Why the parent is the swing factor

Every other question about Just Dial — is the search business declining, will JD Mart scale, is the stock cheap — is ultimately downstream of one decision the minority shareholder does not control: what Reliance does with this asset and its cash. A parent that deploys the hoard, returns capital, or visibly plugs Just Dial into JioMart's flywheel would re-rate the stock overnight. A parent that leaves it as a quiet, cash-hoarding subsidiary keeps the discount alive indefinitely. That is an unusual position for a public company — its fair value depends less on its own execution than on a controlling shareholder's as-yet-undeclared plan. Anyone buying the discount is really underwriting a view on Reliance's intentions.

Financials & valuation

A steady operating core, a big treasury, and a valuation that only makes sense once you split the two.

Read at face value, FY26 looks contradictory: revenue and operating profit up, net profit down. It resolves the moment you separate the operating business from the cash pile — the operating engine grew, treasury income shrank, and the sum fell. The valuation puzzle resolves the same way: the stock is cheap on reported earnings and absurdly cheap once the cash is stripped out — the only question is whether that cheapness is opportunity or trap.

The one number that settles the thesis: net additions to paid campaigns, read against traffic

At an enterprise value of roughly zero, this stock does not need a re-rating of the business to work — it needs the operating business to remain worth something, and it needs the cash to eventually count. So the metric that proves or breaks the case is the trend in active paid campaigns — the count of paying SMEs (~6.32 lakh) — read alongside traffic. As long as paying campaigns keep rising a few percent a year even while traffic drifts down, the long-tail SME franchise is intact and the near-zero EV is a mispricing. The day campaign growth stalls or reverses — the point at which advertisers conclude the leads no longer convert because the searchers have gone — the discount stops being cheap and starts being correct. Campaigns up, traffic holding: opportunity. Campaigns rolling over: value trap. The second, unrelated trigger is a Reliance capital-allocation decision (a dividend, buyback or deployment) that forces the cash to be counted. One is an operating gate; the other is a corporate-action catalyst. The thesis lives at the intersection.

10

The future, the risks, and the bull/bear

A cash-rich survivor of one internet era, asked to prove it belongs in the next.

Just Dial enters FY27 as a profitable, debt-free, cash-drenched platform with a sticky SME advertiser base, a Reliance parent, and a valuation that dares you to call it cheap. What it must prove is that a horizontal directory can stay relevant against Google, the verticals and AI search — and that its enormous cash pile is an asset for shareholders, not just a comfort for its owner. That is the frame for both cases.

The bull case

  • You pay near-zero for the business — cash & investments (~₹5,852 Cr) roughly equal the whole market cap; a profitable ~29.5%-margin platform comes essentially free.
  • Sticky SME franchise — 6.3 lakh advertisers and 54.7 M listings that still grow; the long tail of small businesses is under-served by the giants and keeps paying because leads convert.
  • Cash-upfront, negative-working-capital model — high-margin, low-capital, self-funding; it compounds the cash pile without needing to invest.
  • Reliance optionality — deployment of the cash, a shareholder return, or real O2O/JioMart integration could re-rate the stock sharply.
  • JD Mart adjacency — a free-ish call on monetising the business database a second time in B2B.

The bear case

  • Structural disruption — Google, Maps, vertical apps and now AI search route around a horizontal directory; traffic is already drifting down (−4.7% YoY).
  • Trapped cash — the discount has persisted for years because the market doubts the pile ever reaches minorities; a controlling promoter may prefer to keep it.
  • Profit quality — roughly half of pre-tax profit is treasury income; strip it out and the operating business is much smaller than the headline P/E implies.
  • Second-place in B2B — IndiaMART leads decisively; JD Mart is a challenger fighting entrenched network effects.
  • Governance / thin float — a CFO exit at results, a ~74% promoter stake and minority-shareholder dependence on Reliance's undeclared plans.
The bottom line

Just Dial is two assets wearing one ticker: a shrinking-traffic but still-profitable local-search platform, and a ₹5,852 Cr investment portfolio that the market values at par and the business at nothing. The cheapness is real and unusual — few profitable companies trade below their own cash — but so is the reason for it, because a directory's relevance is exactly what Google, the vertical apps and AI assistants have spent years eroding, and the cash may answer only to Reliance. The next tests are concrete and datable: whether active paid campaigns keep climbing through FY27 as traffic drifts (proof the SME franchise is holding), and whether Reliance makes any move — a dividend, a buyback, a deployment, or a visible JioMart integration — that forces the cash to be counted. Keep the advertisers paying and get the cash working, and a business priced at zero looks like a gift; lose the advertisers to the platforms while the hoard stays locked in a subsidiary, and the discount will have been the market telling the truth all along.
